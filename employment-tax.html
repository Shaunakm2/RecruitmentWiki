Employment Modes & Tax Terms

W2, 1099, C2C, and what "contract" actually means for pay and taxes — the questions every candidate eventually asks.

Simple explanation: W2 means the worker is an employee and taxes are withheld automatically by the employer. 1099 means the worker is an independent contractor responsible for their own taxes. C2C (Corp-to-Corp) means the worker has their own registered company, and that company — not the individual — is paid and handles its own taxes.

Why it matters: Candidates often have a strong preference for one mode, and misunderstanding the difference can cause a placement to fall through late in the process over a tax surprise.

Common mistakes: Assuming C2C and 1099 are the same thing — they're not; C2C requires the candidate to have their own registered business entity.

Simple explanation: Contract is a fixed-term engagement with a defined end date. Contract-to-hire starts as a contract with the option to convert to a full-time employee after a trial period. Direct hire means the candidate becomes a permanent employee from day one.

Why it matters: These terms set expectations about job security and benefits — a candidate deciding between offers will weigh these differences heavily.

Common mistakes: Using "permanent" and "contract-to-hire" interchangeably when describing a role — they carry very different expectations for the candidate.

Simple explanation: W2 pay usually looks lower per hour than 1099 or C2C because the employer is covering payroll taxes and often benefits; 1099/C2C pay looks higher per hour but the worker must set aside money for self-employment taxes and typically has no employer-provided benefits.

Why it matters: Comparing two offers by hourly rate alone, without factoring in taxes and benefits, gives a misleading picture of which one is actually better for the candidate.

Common mistakes: Advising a candidate to just "pick the higher rate" without mentioning the tax and benefits trade-off.

Interview Questions

  • What's the key difference between 1099 and C2C?
  • Why might a candidate's take-home pay be similar across a higher 1099 rate and a lower W2 rate?
  • What does "contract-to-hire" mean for a candidate's job security?

Summary

W2, 1099, and C2C describe who withholds taxes and how a worker is legally engaged. Contract, contract-to-hire, and direct hire describe how long the role is expected to last and whether it converts to permanent. Recruiters who can explain both clearly avoid late-stage surprises that kill placements.
