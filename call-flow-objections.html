Call Flow & Objection Handling

A repeatable structure for a candidate call, and how to respond when they push back.

►PASTE_VIDEO_EMBED_URL_HERE

Watch: Call Flow & Objections

🎧 Listen to the audio version

Simple explanation: A simple structure for a candidate call: Open (introduce yourself and why you're calling), Qualify (confirm skills, availability, and interest), Pitch (describe the role clearly), Close (agree on clear next steps).

Why it matters: Following a consistent structure keeps calls efficient and makes sure you don't forget to confirm key details, like work authorization or rate expectations.

Common mistakes: Pitching the role before qualifying the candidate — you can end up spending ten minutes selling a job to someone who was never a fit or available.

Simple explanation: The most common candidate objections are about rate ("It's below what I'm making now"), location ("I don't want to relocate or commute"), and timing ("I'm not looking to move right now").

Why it matters: These three objections cover the majority of "no" responses recruiters hear — having a prepared response for each makes calls far more effective.

Common mistakes: Treating every objection as a hard "no" instead of a starting point for a conversation.

Simple explanation: Instead of arguing with an objection, restate it as a question to understand what's really behind it — e.g., "It sounds like rate is the main concern — what would make this work for you?"

Why it matters: This uncovers the candidate's real priority (sometimes it isn't the stated objection) and keeps the conversation collaborative instead of adversarial.

Common mistakes: Immediately countering with a rebuttal ("Well actually, the market rate is...") instead of asking a clarifying question first.

Interview Questions

  • What are the four steps of a simple call flow?
  • What are the three most common candidate objections?
  • Why is it better to ask a question in response to an objection rather than argue?

Summary

A good call follows Open, Qualify, Pitch, Close — in that order — and treats objections as information, not roadblocks. Acknowledging an objection and asking a clarifying question almost always gets further than arguing.
