ORS, VMS, MSP & Recruitment Terminology

The systems and shorthand every US Staffing recruiter runs into in their first week on the job.

Simple explanation: ORS is a staffing team, usually based in a country like India or the Philippines, that sources and screens candidates for US-based jobs during hours that overlap with the US workday. They work the "night shift" locally so US clients get coverage during their own daytime.

Why it matters: A lot of your work as a new recruiter may literally be ORS work. Understanding the term helps you see where you fit in the bigger picture: offshore teams usually source and screen, while onshore (US-based) recruiters close, negotiate, and manage the client relationship.

Common mistakes: Assuming ORS work doesn't require knowing US compliance, work authorization, or culture. It does — a bad submittal (wrong visa status, unrealistic rate, poor English communication) wastes the whole delivery chain's time and can cost the agency the client.

Simple explanation: A VMS is a website (like Fieldglass or Beeline) that big companies use to run their contract-hiring process. Job openings, candidate submittals, timesheets, and invoices all flow through it instead of email.

Why it matters: If a client uses a VMS, emailing a resume to the hiring manager isn't enough — the candidate must be formally submitted through the VMS by a specific deadline, with specific fields filled in correctly. Miss the window and even a perfect candidate is out.

Common mistakes: Submitting a candidate above the client's rate ceiling. Most VMS platforms auto-reject over-rate submittals before a human ever sees them, so always confirm the rate cap before you submit.

Simple explanation: An MSP is a company a large employer hires to manage its entire contract-workforce program: which staffing agencies ("suppliers") are allowed to submit candidates, what the rules are, and often the VMS itself.

Why it matters: When a client has an MSP, your agency is one of several approved suppliers competing for the same job orders under the same rules. The MSP — not the hiring manager — usually controls things like rate caps and how many candidates each supplier can submit per requisition.

Common mistakes: Mixing up MSP and VMS. The VMS is the software; the MSP is the company and program that decides how that software is used. Also, trying to go around the MSP straight to the hiring manager — this usually breaks the program's rules and can get your agency removed from the vendor list entirely.

Simple explanation: A handful of terms come up in nearly every conversation. Learn these five first:

  • Job Order (Req): An open position a client wants filled — the starting point of a recruiter's day.
  • Submittal: Sending a candidate's profile to the client (or into the VMS) for consideration against a specific job order.
  • Placement: A submittal that actually resulted in the candidate starting the job — the core metric recruiters are measured on.
  • Bench: Consultants already employed by the staffing firm (often on a visa like H1B) who aren't yet billing on a client project. The firm pays them while sourcing new work.
  • Bill Rate vs. Pay Rate: The bill rate is what the client pays the agency per hour; the pay rate is what the agency pays the consultant. The gap between the two is the agency's margin.

Why it matters: These words are shorthand every recruiter, account manager, and client uses daily. Not knowing them slows down every call and email.

Common mistakes: Confusing bill rate and pay rate when discussing compensation — quoting the wrong number to a candidate damages trust fast, and quoting it to a client can cost the deal.

Summary

ORS, VMS, and MSP describe three different layers of how US staffing actually runs: who sources the candidate (ORS), what system the submittal goes through (VMS), and who sets the rules for that whole vendor program (MSP). Layer the core glossary — job order, submittal, placement, bench, bill rate vs. pay rate — on top, and you can follow almost any staffing conversation from day one.
