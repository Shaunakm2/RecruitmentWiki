Sourcing & Boolean Search

Where to find candidates, and how to write search strings that actually narrow results instead of drowning you in noise.

Simple explanation: LinkedIn Recruiter, Indeed, Dice, Monster, and CareerBuilder are the most common places US staffing recruiters search for candidates, each with different strengths — Dice for IT/tech, Indeed for broad volume, LinkedIn for passive candidates.

Why it matters: Picking the right source for the role saves hours — searching Dice for a warehouse role, or Indeed for a niche cybersecurity architect, both waste time.

Common mistakes: Always defaulting to the same one or two sources out of habit, even when the role calls for a different platform.

Simple explanation: Boolean search uses AND, OR, NOT, quotation marks, and parentheses to combine or exclude search terms — AND narrows results, OR broadens them, NOT excludes a term, and quotes search for an exact phrase.

Why it matters: A well-built Boolean string turns thousands of irrelevant resumes into a short, relevant list — it's one of the highest-leverage skills in sourcing.

Common mistakes: Overusing OR and ending up with a search so broad it returns everything, or overusing AND and quotes and ending up with zero results.

Simple explanation: A basic pattern is: (job title OR synonym) AND (skill1 OR skill2) AND "location" NOT (unwanted term). For example: ("java developer" OR "java engineer") AND (spring OR hibernate) AND "Dallas" NOT "manager".

Why it matters: Having a repeatable template means you're not starting from scratch on every new job order.

Common mistakes: Forgetting to add a NOT clause for common false positives, like excluding "manager" or "intern" when sourcing individual-contributor roles.

Summary

Sourcing well means picking the right platform for the role and writing a Boolean string that's neither too broad nor too narrow. Start from a repeatable template — title OR synonyms, AND required skills, AND location, NOT common false positives — and adjust per job order.
