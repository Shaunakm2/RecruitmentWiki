US Culture & Geography

Enough US geography, time zones, and workplace culture to sound confident on your very first call to an American candidate.

Simple explanation: The US has 50 states grouped into regions (Northeast, South, Midwest, West), and four continental time zones — Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific — plus Alaska and Hawaii further west.

Why it matters: Knowing roughly where a candidate or client is located tells you what time zone to call them in, and recognizing regional context (e.g., "Bay Area" means West Coast, Pacific Time, tech hub) helps a conversation feel natural.

Common mistakes: Calling a candidate in Eastern Time at what feels like a reasonable hour in your own time zone, without checking the difference first — a common way recruiters accidentally call at 5am or during dinner.

Simple explanation: The US observes federal holidays (New Year's Day, MLK Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas) when many offices close, and Daylight Saving Time shifts clocks forward in March and back in November across most of the country.

Why it matters: Client offices and candidates are often unreachable on federal holidays, and forgetting a DST shift can throw off a scheduled call by a full hour.

Common mistakes: Scheduling interviews around Thanksgiving week or the last week of December without checking availability first — response times slow dramatically during these periods.

Simple explanation: US business communication tends to be direct and time-conscious, even in professional settings — first names are common, and getting to the point quickly is valued over lengthy pleasantries.

Why it matters: Recruiters from cultures with more formal or indirect communication styles can come across as overly deferential or vague, which sometimes reads as unprepared rather than polite.

Common mistakes: Over-apologizing or hedging heavily ("I'm so sorry to bother you, if it's okay, maybe...") instead of stating the purpose of the call clearly and confidently.

Interview Questions

  • Why does time zone awareness matter when scheduling a candidate call?
  • Name two US federal holidays when most offices will be closed.

Summary

A recruiter who knows US time zones, federal holidays, and communication norms avoids the most common early mistakes: calling at the wrong hour, scheduling around a holiday, or coming across as unprepared on a first call. None of this requires deep cultural expertise — just a working map and a direct, confident tone.
